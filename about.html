About The Sharkive

A shared, photo-based record of the sharks landed and sold at markets and roadside stalls in Seychelles. It's built so that, over time, the data can show which species are being caught, where, and whether that is changing.

Why it matters

Sharks grow slowly, mature late and have few young, so their populations recover slowly once they decline. Many of the species found in the western Indian Ocean are listed as threatened on the IUCN Red List.

Catches from small-scale and artisanal fishing, the sharks you see on a market table or at the roadside, are often not recorded in any detail. Without that information it's hard to know which species are being caught, how big they are, or whether things are getting better or worse.

What we hope the data will do

The aim is to build a long-term record that can, one day, be used to:

  • Show which species are landed, including how often threatened species turn up.
  • Track change over time: in the species mix, in the sizes of sharks landed, and across seasons and years.
  • Map where sharks are landed and sold around the islands.
  • Support conservation and fisheries management by sharing the data with researchers, conservation groups and the relevant authorities.

Conclusions about populations need a lot of data collected consistently over a long time. Every record adds to that, and the more people who log what they see, the more useful it becomes.

How it works

  1. Photograph a shark you see for sale or landed.
  2. When and where are recorded automatically from the photo itself, so every record has a reliable date, time and location. They can't be typed in or changed.
  3. A species suggestion is made on your phone by comparing the photo with photos that have already been confirmed. It is a matching tool, not a generative AI, and it improves as more photos are confirmed.
  4. A reviewer checks every record and confirms the species before it's added to the database.
  5. Confirmed records are published on the Records page, with a map and a downloadable data file.

Taking a useful photo

  • Show the whole shark from the side if you can, with the fins and tail visible. The shape of the head, the dorsal fins and the tail markings are the main clues to the species.
  • Use the in-app camera, or an original photo from your camera roll. Photos forwarded through WhatsApp or Messenger lose their time and location and can't be used.
  • Enter the number of sharks in the photo. If there are several species, log each one separately.
  • Frame the shark, not people. Please avoid including faces.
  • Always ask before photographing someone's stall or catch.

This is about sharks, not people

The Sharkive records what is caught. It isn't about who caught or sold it. It doesn't collect the names of fishers or vendors, and it isn't an enforcement tool. Fishers and vendors know more about sharks than almost anyone, and their cooperation is what makes a record like this possible.

Your data

  • No account is needed to log a shark, and no personal details are collected.
  • Each record stores the photo, when and where it was taken, the place name and details you add, and the type of device used.
  • Published records show the photo, species, date, time and location.

Who runs it

The Sharkive is an independent citizen-science project run from Praslin, Seychelles.
