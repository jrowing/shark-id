Log sharks and rays

At a market or roadside seller

Start a visit when you arrive. Each visit is recorded, even if there are no sharks or rays. Knowing where people looked and found none is what lets the data show how often they really turn up.

The time and place of the visit come from your phone's clock and GPS.

A live shark or ray

Log a shark or ray

Photograph the whole animal side-on if you can (rays from above), with the fins and tail visible. The time and place are taken from the photo automatically, and you can't change them.

Existing photos must be originals from the camera roll. Screenshots and photos forwarded by WhatsApp or Messenger have no capture data and will be refused. Underwater camera photos usually have no GPS, so they can only be logged within 30 minutes of taking them (the phone's location is used).

Sex
How to tell

Look at the underside, just behind the pelvic fins (the pair of small fins near the vent). Males have two stiff, rod-like claspers. Females have none. Rays are the same: claspers sit either side of the base of the tail. Young males have short claspers that are easy to miss, so choose Can't tell if you're not sure.

Put a Sharkive scale card, a tape or a sheet of A4 flat beside the animal before you take the photo, then tap its ends and the animal's ends.
